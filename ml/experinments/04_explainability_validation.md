# Explainability Validation Experiments

## Objective
The objective of these experiments is to evaluate whether feature-level explanations produced by the proposed crop yield forecasting system are stable, interpretable, and practically useful. The goal is to distinguish meaningful explanations from purely decorative or unstable attribution outputs.

---

## Hypothesis
Feature-level explanations generated by the proposed system will remain stable under repeated evaluation, exhibit meaningful sensitivity to input perturbations, and provide actionable insight into model behavior without degrading predictive performance.

---

## Explanation Method
Feature-level explanations are generated using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations). SHAP is selected due to its model-agnostic formulation and widespread adoption in prior machine learning interpretability studies.

---

## Explanation Scope
Explainability analysis is restricted to features directly exposed to end users and decision-makers. Internal or derived features not visible at the decision-support interface are excluded to maintain interpretability relevance.

---

## Stability Evaluation
Explanation stability is evaluated by computing SHAP values for identical input instances across multiple runs with fixed model parameters.

Stability is assessed using:
- Rank correlation of top contributing features
- Variance of SHAP attribution magnitudes

Unstable explanations are identified and documented.

---

## Sensitivity Evaluation
To evaluate whether explanations respond meaningfully to input changes, controlled perturbations are applied to individual features while holding all other inputs constant.

Changes in SHAP attributions are analyzed relative to the magnitude of input perturbations to assess explanation sensitivity.

---

## Feature Ablation Analysis
Feature ablation experiments are conducted by systematically removing the top-k features identified by SHAP and retraining or re-evaluating the model.

Performance degradation resulting from feature removal is measured to assess whether highly ranked features materially influence predictions.

---

## Domain Plausibility Assessment
Explanation outputs are qualitatively evaluated for consistency with established agronomic understanding. Features identified as dominant contributors are examined for physical plausibility based on domain knowledge.

Inconsistencies are documented and discussed rather than corrected.

---

## Evaluation Criteria
Explainability is considered valid if the following conditions are met:
- Feature rankings are stable across repeated evaluations
- Explanation sensitivity aligns with input perturbations
- Removal of top-ranked features results in measurable performance degradation
- Identified features exhibit domain plausibility

Failure to meet these criteria is explicitly reported.

---

## Expected Failure Modes
The following failure modes are anticipated and monitored:

- High variance in feature rankings across runs
- Dominant attribution to weakly relevant features
- Minimal performance impact after removal of highly ranked features

Such outcomes are treated as negative findings.

---

## Non-Claims
- No claim of causal attribution is made.
- No claim that explainability improves predictive accuracy.
- No claim that explanations reflect true physical processes.

---

## Experimental Status
This document specifies the explainability validation protocol prior to execution. No explainability results are included at this stage. All analyses are performed following the procedures defined herein to prevent post-hoc interpretation.
